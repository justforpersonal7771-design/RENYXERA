### Chapter 00 · Math Toolbox for Algorithms *(New Prerequisite Chapter)*

#### Lesson 00.1 · Logarithms, Exponents & Essential Algorithm Series
- **Tag:** `[MUST]` — Zero-level readers cannot parse `2^{\log n}`, `n^{\log_4 5}`, `\log(n!)`, or loop sums without this warm-up (`2026_FN_Q17`, `2026_AN_Q24`, `2021_FN_Q61`, `2017_AN_Q61`).
- **Estimated reading time:** 55 minutes.
- **Prerequisites:** High-school powers (`2^3 = 8`).
- **Beginner must-see:** Think of $\log_2 n$ simply as **"How many times can I cut $n$ in half until I hit 1?"** For $n = 1024$, cutting in half 10 times hits `1`, so $\log_2 1024 = 10$! That explains why $2^{\log_2 n}$ literally undoes the cut and gives $n$ back (`2026_AN_Q24`)!
- **Finer points:**
  - **6 Log/Exponent Rules:** $b^{\log_b n} = n$, **Swap identity $a^{\log_b c} = c^{\log_b a}$**, $\log_a n = \frac{\log_2 n}{\log_2 a}$, $\log(xy) = \log x + \log y$, $\log(n^k) = k \log n$, **Stirling's Approximation $\log_2(n!) = \Theta(n \log n)$** (`2026_AN_Q24`).
  - **Comparing exponents:** $n^{\log_4 5}$ vs $n^2$ (`4^2 = 16 > 5`, so $\log_4 5 < 2$, `2026_FN_Q17`!); taking $\log$ of both sides to compare `n^{\log n}` vs `n^{\sqrt{n}}` vs `10^n` (`2021_FN_Q61`).
  - **5 Essential Summations:**
    1. Arithmetic: $\sum_{i=1}^n i = \frac{n(n+1)}{2} = \Theta(n^2)$.
    2. Shrinking Geometric (`r < 1`): $n + n/2 + n/4 + \dots = \Theta(n)$ (`2023_FN_Q54`).
    3. Growing Geometric (`r > 1`): $1 + 2 + 4 + \dots + 2^k = 2^{k+1}-1 = \Theta(2^k)$.
    4. Arithmetico-Geometric: $\sum_{k=1}^n k \cdot 2^n$ vs $\sum_{k=1}^n k / 2^k$ (`2025_FN_Q20`).
    5. Harmonic: $H_n = \sum_{i=1}^n \frac{1}{i} = \ln n + O(1) = \Theta(\log n)$ (`2017_AN_Q61`).
- **GATE-level items:** Ranking `n^{1/3}, log n, log(n!), 2^{log n}` (`2026_AN_Q24`), `n^{\log n}, n^{\sqrt{n}}, 10^n` (`2021_FN_Q61`), and `100/n, 10, log n, sqrt(n), n` (`2017_FN_Q21`).
- **Diagrams needed:** `ARRAY` + `COMPARE` (Staircase AP box, Shrinking GP unit square, and Harmonic area bars); `TRACE` (Taking logs step-by-step to rank `f_1, f_2, f_3` in `2021_FN_Q61`).
- **Hand traces:** Evaluating `2^{\log_2 32}`, comparing `n^{\log_4 5}` vs `n^2`, summing `n + n/2 + ...` for `n = 16`, and simplifying $\log(n!)$ via pairing $1 \times n$.
- **Common mistakes:** Thinking `2^{\log n}` is exponential (`2^{\log_2 n} = n` linear! `2026_AN_Q24`); thinking `n + n/2 + n/4 + ...` is $n \log n$ instead of $2n = \Theta(n)$ (`2023_FN_Q54`); thinking `log(n!)` is $\Theta(\log n)$ (`2026_AN_Q24`).

### Chapter 01 · Asymptotics, Loop Complexity and Recurrences

#### Lesson 01.1 · Asymptotic Notations (`O, \Omega, \Theta, o, \omega`) & Space vs Time Complexity
- **Tag:** `[MUST]` — Foundation of `19/73` Algorithms PYQs (`2024_FN_Q20`, `2023_FN_Q29`, `2022_FN_Q11`, `2021_FN_Q61`, `2017_FN_Q21`).
- **Estimated reading time:** 65 minutes.
- **Prerequisites:** Lesson 00.1.
- **Beginner must-see:** Big-O is like saying *"at most (`<=`)"* for very large inputs, $\Omega$ is *"at least (`>=`)"*, and $\Theta$ is *"exact tight fit (`==`)"* — ignoring constant multipliers like `100n` vs `2n` (`2023_FN_Q54`)! Watch out: **Worst-Case** is WHICH input you test (the nastiest one!), while **$O / \Omega / \Theta$** are just math symbols describing that curve (`2024_FN_Q20`)!
- **Finer points:**
  - Formal definitions (`\exists c, n_0` vs `\forall c \exists n_0` for `o, \omega`).
  - **The Limit Ratio Test:** $\lim_{n\to\infty} \frac{f(n)}{g(n)}$ (`0 => o(g) and O(g)`, `finite > 0 => \Theta(g)`, `\infty => \omega(g) and \Omega(g)`).
  - **5 Classic Traps:**
    - If $f = \Theta(g)$, then $f = O(g)$ AND $f = \Omega(g)$ (`2024_FN_Q20`).
    - $n \in o(n^2)$ AND $n \in O(n^2)$, NOT $\Theta(n^2)$ (`2023_FN_Q29`).
    - Polynomial vs Exponential under $f(n^2)$ vs $f(n)^2$ (`2022_FN_Q11`: holds for polynomials, fails for $2^n$!).
    - Decreasing function `100/n` is $o(1)$ — strictly smaller than constant `10` (`2017_FN_Q21`!).
    - Constants $c$ CANNOT depend on $n$, and $f(n) = 2^{n+1}$ IS $\Theta(2^n)$, whereas $2^{2n} = 4^n \neq O(2^n)$!
  - **Time vs Auxiliary Space Complexity:** Recursion call-stack depth counts toward space complexity!
- **GATE-level items:** Ranking growth functions (`2026_AN_Q24`, `2021_FN_Q61`, `2017_FN_Q21`), `O/o/\Omega/\Theta` MSQs (`2023_FN_Q29`), worst-case `O(N) & \Omega(N)` (`2024_FN_Q20`), and `f(n^2)` transformations (`2022_FN_Q11`).
- **Diagrams needed:** `COMPARE` (`2 × 3` Scenarios vs Bounds grid + 5 speed meters `o, O, \Theta, \Omega, \omega`); `FLOW` (Limit-Ratio test decision tree).
- **Hand traces:** Testing $f(n)=n, g(n)=n^2$ (`2023_FN_Q29`), $f(n^2)$ for $n^3$ vs $2^n$ (`2022_FN_Q11`), and limit-comparing $n^{\log n}$ vs $n^{\sqrt{n}}$ (`2021_FN_Q61`).
- **Common mistakes:** Confusing $\Omega$ with "Best Case" (`2024_FN_Q20`!); thinking $f \in o(g)$ prevents $f \in O(g)$ (`2023_FN_Q29`!); claiming $2^{2n} = \Theta(2^n)$; forgetting recursion stack in space complexity.

#### Lesson 01.2 · Loop Complexity Analysis (Translating Code into Sums)
- **Tag:** `[MUST]` — High-ROI guaranteed questions (`2023_FN_Q54`, `2018_FN_Q42`, `2018_FN_Q55`, `2017_AN_Q61`).
- **Estimated reading time:** 60 minutes.
- **Prerequisites:** Lesson 00.1, Lesson 01.1, C5 (loops).
- **Beginner must-see:** To find how fast a loop runs, don't guess "2 nested loops = $n^2$!" Instead, ask ONE simple question: **for each step of the outer loop (`i = 1, 2, 3...`), how many steps does the inner loop actually take?** Then add those counts up!
- **Finer points:**
  - Single loops: `i++` ($n$), `i += k` ($n/k$), `i *= 2` ($\log n$), `i = i * i` ($\log\log n$), `i*i <= n` ($\sqrt{n}$).
  - Dependent nested loops: `j <= i` ($\Theta(n^2)$), `n` then `n/2` then `n/4` ($\Theta(n)$, `2023_FN_Q54`!), **`j += i` ($\sum n/i = \Theta(n \log n)$, `2017_AN_Q61`!)**, sequential halving loops ($\log n$ then $\log\log n$, `2018_FN_Q42`!).
  - Multi-parameter recursive loops: `Count(x, y)` (`2018_FN_Q55`: `y` drops by 1 -> `1023` outer rounds × `10` halvings of `x`!).
  - Early exit worst-case (`2024_FN_Q20`): check input that delays `break`/`return` until the very last element!
- **GATE-level items:** Computing exact execution counts (`2018_FN_Q42`, `2018_FN_Q55`) and tight $\Theta$ bounds on nested halving/harmonic loops (`2023_FN_Q54`, `2017_AN_Q61`).
- **Diagrams needed:** `TRACE` (6-pattern Loop Trace Table: `i` value, inner count, total sum); `CALLTREE` (`Count(1024, 1024)` showing `1023` resets of `x=1024 -> 1`, `2018_FN_Q55`).
- **Hand traces:** Tracing `2023_FN_Q54`, `2017_AN_Q61` (`n=8`: `8 + 4 + 3 + 2 + 2 + 2 + 2 + 2`), `2018_FN_Q42` (`5`), and `2018_FN_Q55` (`10230`).
- **Common mistakes:** Blindly writing $\Theta(n^2)$ for two nested `for` loops (`2017_AN_Q61` is $n \log n$!) or $\Theta(n \log n)$ for `while(n > 1) { for(1..n); n /= 2; }` (`2023_FN_Q54` is $\Theta(n)$!); miscounting `y != 1` off by 1 (`1023` vs `1024` in `2018_FN_Q55`!).

#### Lesson 01.3 · Solving Recurrences: Master Theorem, Recursion Trees, Unrolling & Variable Substitution
- **Tag:** `[MUST]` — Tested across nearly every GATE paper (`2026_FN_Q17`, `2026_AN_Q25`, `2025_FN_Q20`, `2024_FN_Q57`, `2021_FN_Q50`, `2021_AN_Q55`, `2020_FN_Q34`, `2017_AN_Q50`).
- **Estimated reading time:** 90 minutes.
- **Prerequisites:** Lesson 00.1, Lesson 01.1, DS D5 (Recursion Trees).
- **Beginner must-see:** Master Theorem is just a tug-of-war between **Leaves** (total subcalls $n^{\log_b a}$) and **Root work** ($f(n)$)! Whoever is polynomially bigger wins; if they tie, multiply by $\log n$! And when a weird recurrence breaks Master Theorem (`T(n-1)`, `T(\sqrt{n})`, or `T(n/2) + T(2n/5)`), just draw the **Recursion Tree** or swap **`n = 2^m`**!
- **Finer points:**
  - **Master Theorem ($T(n) = a T(n/b) + f(n)$, $a \ge 1, b > 1$):**
    - Compare $n^{\log_b a}$ vs $f(n)$: Case 1 (polynomially smaller -> $n^{\log_b a}$, `2021_AN_Q55(C)`), Case 2 (including $\log^k n$ -> $n^{\log_b a} \log^{k+1} n$), Case 3 (polynomially larger + regularity condition -> $f(n)$).
    - **Master Theorem Traps:** $n / \log n$ sits in the non-polynomial gap between Case 1 & 2 (`2021_AN_Q55(B)` -> $\Theta(n \log \log n)$ for $2T(n/2) + n/\log n$!); floors/ceilings inside `T(\lceil n/b \rceil)` don't change $\Theta$; **Coupled recurrences `T_1, T_2`** solved bottom-up (`2026_FN_Q17`).
  - **Linear Recurrence Unrolling (`T(n - c)`):** `T(n-1) + 1 -> \Theta(n)`, `T(n-1) + n -> \Theta(n^2)` (`2026_AN_Q25`), **`T(n) = 2T(n-1) + n 2^n -> \Theta(n^2 2^n)` (`2025_FN_Q20`)**!
  - **Unequal-Split Recursion Tree & Akra-Bazzi Intuition (`2021_FN_Q50`):** $T(\lceil n/2 \rceil) + T(\lfloor 2n/5 \rfloor) + 7n$: level sum decays by $9/10 < 1 \implies \Theta(n)$! (Contrast with `n/3 + 2n/3 = 1 \implies \Theta(n \log n)`!).
  - **Change-of-Variable Substitution (`\sqrt{n}`, `n^{1/a}`):**
    - $T(n) = 2 T(\sqrt{n}) + 1$ (`n = 2^m -> S(m) = 2 S(m/2) + 1 -> \Theta(\log n)`, `2017_AN_Q50`).
    - Divide first! $T(n) = \sqrt{n} T(\sqrt{n}) + n \implies \frac{T(n)}{n} = \frac{T(\sqrt{n})}{\sqrt{n}} + 1 \implies \Theta(n \log \log n)$ (`2024_FN_Q57`!).
    - $T(n) = T(n^{1/a}) + 1 \implies \Theta(\log_a \log_b n)$ (`2020_FN_Q34`!).
  - **Amortized Analysis Intuition (`[SHOULD]`):** Aggregate & Potential method on Binary Counter increment ($O(1)$ amortized).
- **GATE-level items:** Coupled Master Theorem (`2026_FN_Q17`), non-polynomial gap (`2021_AN_Q55`), unequal splits (`2021_FN_Q50`), $\sqrt{n}$ substitution (`2024_FN_Q57`, `2017_AN_Q50`), $n^{1/a}$ (`2020_FN_Q34`), and exponential unrolling (`2025_FN_Q20`).
- **Diagrams needed:** `TREE` (3 Master Theorem tug-of-war trees + unequal `9/10` decaying tree for `2021_FN_Q50`); `FLOW` (Master Theorem + Change-of-Variable selector flowchart); `TRACE` (Unrolling `2T(n-1) + n 2^n` level by level, `2025_FN_Q20`).
- **Hand traces:** Solving ALL 6 styles (`2026_FN_Q17`, `2025_FN_Q20`, `2024_FN_Q57`, `2021_FN_Q50`, `2020_FN_Q34`, `2017_AN_Q50`) line by line.
- **Common mistakes:** Applying Master Case 2 when $f(n) = n / \log n$ (`2021_AN_Q55(B)`!); forgetting `n` division before substituting $n = 2^m$ in `2024_FN_Q57`; missing the extra `n` factor in $T(n) = 2T(n-1) + n 2^n$ (`2025_FN_Q20` gives $n 2^n$ vs $n^2 2^n$!); forgetting `\log n` in Case 2 (`2021_AN_Q55(D)`!).

Even weird half-sorted arrays like **Bitonic mountains** (`2025_AN_Q41`) or `00001111` transition arrays (`2017_FN_Q45`) still slice in half using $O(\log n)$ probes!
- **Finer points:**
  - **Binary Search Exact Bounds (`2021_AN_Q31`, `2017_FN_Q45`):**
    - Overflow-safe mid: `mid = low + (high - low) / 2`.
    - Worst-case probes for `n` elements: `floor(log_2 n) + 1` = `ceil(log_2(n + 1))` (`31` elements -> `5` probes, `2017_FN_Q45`!).
    - Variants: First `1` in `0...01...1`, lower/upper bound (`while (low < high)` termination check!), rotated sorted array.
    - Why **Ternary Search** (`2 log_3 n ≈ 1.26 log_2 n`) does MORE comparisons than Binary Search!
  - **Bitonic Array Peak & Search (`2025_AN_Q41`):** Compare `A[mid]` vs `A[mid + 1]` to find peak in $\Theta(\log n)$, then binary-search ascending left & descending right in $\Theta(\log n)$!
  - **Minimum Comparisons for Selection Tricks:**
    - Find ANY non-maximum in distinct unsorted list (`2025_AN_Q20`): compare any 2 elements, pick smaller = **1 comparison**!
    - **Tournament / Pairwise Min & Max (`2021_FN_Q30`):** Process elements in pairs (1 compare between pair, 1 vs current max, 1 vs current min = 3 per 2 items!) -> **$3\lceil n/2 \rceil - 2$ comparisons**!
    - **Randomized Quickselect (Expected $O(n)$, Worst $O(n^2)$)** vs **Median-of-Medians BFPRT (`2019_FN_Q61`):** $T(n) = T(n/5) + T(7n/10) + O(n) = \Theta(n)$ worst case! (Finding median of each of $n$ arrays of size $n$ = $n \times O(n) = O(n^2)$ total, `2019_FN_Q61`!).
- **GATE-level items:** Exact `5` probes for `31` elements (`2017_FN_Q45`), `3\lceil n/2 \rceil - 2` min-max upper bound (`2021_FN_Q30`), `1` comparison for non-largest (`2025_AN_Q20`), $\Theta(\log n)$ bitonic search (`2025_AN_Q41`), and $O(n^2)$ median-of-$n$-arrays (`2019_FN_Q61`).
- **Diagrams needed:** `FRAMES` (31-slot `0..01..1` halving `16 -> 8 -> 4 -> 2 -> 1`); `ARRAY` (Bitonic mountain peak probe `A[mid] < A[mid+1]`); `COMPARE` (Naive `2n-2` vs Pairwise `3n/2 - 2` Min-Max pairing).
- **Hand traces:** Tracing `2017_FN_Q45` (`low=0, high=30`), tracing pairwise min-max on `[7, 2, 9, 4, 1, 8]` (`7` comparisons instead of `10`), and counting comparisons on `2019_FN_Q61`.
- **Common mistakes:** Answering `N - 1` instead of `1` for finding *any* non-largest element (`2025_AN_Q20`!); writing `ceil(log_2(31))`? Note `ceil(log_2 31) = 5` and `floor(log_2 31) + 1 = 5` agree, yet for `n = 32` it's `6`, NOT `5` (`ceil(log_2(n+1))` works for ALL `n`!); forgetting `mid = (low + high)/2` overflows 32-bit `int`.

#### Lesson 02.2 · Comparison Sorts: Elementary (`Bubble`, `Insertion`, `Selection`) & $O(n \log n)$ (`Merge`, `Heap`, `Quick`)
- **Tag:** `[MUST]` — High-yield tracing & algorithmic intuition (`2026_AN_Q32`, `2025_FN_Q33`, `2021_FN_Q25`, `2019_FN_Q31`, `2017_AN_Q19`).
- **Estimated reading time:** 85 minutes.
- **Prerequisites:** Lesson 01.3, DS Arrays & Heaps (`D9`).
- **Beginner must-see:** Simple sorts (`Bubble`, `Insertion`, `Selection`) check neighbours or scan for the smallest item ($O(n^2)$), while clever splits (**Merge Sort**, **Quicksort**) or a **Max-Heap** (**Heap Sort**) shrink work to $O(n \log n)$! Notice how **nearly-sorted** arrays change everything: Insertion Sort finishes in almost linear $O(n)$ (`2021_FN_Q25`) while naive last-pivot Quicksort crashes to $O(n^2)$!
- **Finer points:**
  - **Inversions (`i < j` with `A[i] > A[j]`):**
    - Both Bubble Sort and Insertion Sort perform **exact # of inversions** swaps/shifts (worst case descending: $\binom{n}{2} = \frac{n(n-1)}{2}$, `2025_FN_Q33`: $\binom{30}{2} = 435$!).
    - Selection Sort takes $\Theta(n^2)$ comparisons ALWAYS, yet only **$O(n)$ swaps**!
  - **Insertion Sort Adaptive Advantage (`2021_FN_Q25`):** Best case sorted = $n - 1$ comparisons ($\Theta(n)$); nearly sorted ($O(n)$ inversions) beats Merge, Heap, and Quicksort!
  - **Merge Sort (`2026_AN_Q32`):** Always $\Theta(n \log n)$ worst case; needs $O(n)$ auxiliary space; **Void Merge** happens when `max(L) <= min(R)` (`2026_AN_Q32`: `3` void merges!).
  - **Heap Sort (`2017_AN_Q19`):** `build-heap` ($O(n)$) + $n-1$ `extract-max`s ($O(n \log n)$) = $\Theta(n \log n)$ worst case, $O(1)$ space, **UNSTABLE**!
  - **Quicksort:**
    - **Lomuto** (single directional scan, pivot at end) vs **Hoare** (two pointers moving inward).
    - **Last-element pivot trap (`2021_FN_Q25(D)`):** Sorted/nearly-sorted input triggers $\Theta(n^2)$!
    - **Randomized Quicksort (`2019_FN_Q31`):** Expected $\Theta(n \log n)$; probability of picking worst pivot (smallest or largest of $n$ distinct items) = **`2 / n`** (`2/25 = 0.08`, `2019_FN_Q31`)!
    - Stack space: worst $O(n)$, balanced/tail-call $O(\log n)$.
- **GATE-level items:** Void merges in Merge Sort (`2026_AN_Q32`), descending swaps (`2025_FN_Q33`), sorting a nearly-sorted array (`2021_FN_Q25`), worst-pivot probability (`2019_FN_Q31`), and Heap Sort worst-case (`2017_AN_Q19`).
- **Diagrams needed:** `TREE` (Merge Sort recursion tree on `[10,7,8,19,41,35,25,31]` circling `L_max <= R_min` void merges, `2026_AN_Q32`); `FRAMES` (Lomuto vs Hoare partitioning step by step); `ARRAY` (Inversion table showing Insertion vs Selection vs Last-Pivot Quicksort on `[1, 2, 3, 5, 4, 6]`).
- **Hand traces:** Tracing `2026_AN_Q32` (`3` void merges), `2025_FN_Q33` (`435`), `2019_FN_Q31` (`2/25 = 0.08`), and comparing Lomuto vs Hoare on `[4, 4, 4, 4]`!
- **Common mistakes:** Thinking `build-heap` makes Heap Sort $O(n)$ total (`extract-max` loop still costs $n \log n$!); thinking Quicksort is $O(n \log n)$ worst-case or fast on already-sorted arrays with last-element pivot (`2021_FN_Q25`); saying `1/n` instead of `2/n` for worst pivot (`both` min and max give $0 : n-1$ split, `2019_FN_Q31`!).

#### Lesson 02.3 · Comparison Lower Bound ($\Omega(n \log n)$), Stability, Linear Sorts & $k$-Way Merge
- **Tag:** `[SHOULD]` — Completes sorting theory, stability MSQs, and non-comparison linear sorting.
- **Estimated reading time:** 60 minutes.
- **Prerequisites:** Lesson 00.1 ($\log(n!) = \Theta(n \log n)$), Lesson 02.2.
- **Beginner must-see:** ANY sorting algorithm that only asks `"Is A[i] < A[j]?"` must tell apart all $n!$ possible input shuffles! A Yes/No question tree with $n!$ leaves MUST have height at least $\log_2(n!) = \Omega(n \log n)$ — **nobody can ever beat $n \log n$ using comparisons alone!** You can only sort in $O(n)$ (**Counting / Radix / Bucket Sort**) by peeking *inside* the digits/values directly!
- **Finer points:**
  - **Decision Tree Proof:** $2^h \ge n! \implies h \ge \lceil \log_2(n!) \rceil = \Omega(n \log n)$.
  - **Stability & In-Place:** Why Merge, Insertion, Bubble, Counting, and Radix are **Stable**, while Selection, Heap, and Quicksort are **Unstable** (and how tagging original indices makes ANY sort stable in $O(n)$ extra space!).
  - **Linear Sorts:**
    - **Counting Sort:** $O(n + k)$ time/space (only good when key range $k = O(n)$).
    - **Radix Sort:** Sorts digit-by-digit from **Least Significant Digit (LSD)** to MSB using a **STABLE** inner sort ($O(d(n + b))$).
    - **Bucket Sort:** $O(n)$ expected on uniform distributions in $[0, 1)$.
  - **$k$-Way Merge (`O(N log k)`):** Merging $k$ sorted lists of total $N$ items using a Min-Heap of size $k$.
  - **Master Sorting Summary Table:** Comparing all 9 algorithms across Best, Average, Worst, Aux Space, Stable, In-Place, Adaptive.
- **GATE-level items:** Identifying stability/in-place/complexity combinations; choosing Counting/Radix when keys lie in `1..n^2` (`base n` Radix takes $O(n)$!); min worst-case comparisons for sorting $n = 4$ or $5$ items (`ceil(log_2(5!)) = 7`).
- **Diagrams needed:** `TREE` (Full 3-element Comparison Decision Tree with `3! = 6` leaf permutations); `ARRAY` (LSD Radix Sort 2-pass stability trace); `COMPARE` (Master 9-algorithm Sorting Matrix).
- **Hand traces:** Tracing LSD Radix Sort on `[329, 457, 657, 839, 436, 720, 355]` and showing why unstable inner sort breaks it; tracing Decision Tree height for `n = 3, 4`.
- **Common mistakes:** Running Radix Sort from MSB without recursive bucketing, or using an unstable inner sort; thinking Counting Sort works in $O(n)$ when keys range up to $2^n$; confusing `Stable` with `Adaptive`.

#### Lesson 03.1 · Probing, Expected Bucket Counts, Universal Hashing & Dynamic Trie Hashing
- **Tag:** `[MUST]` — High-yield problem solver (`2026_FN_Q24`, `2026_AN_Q30`, `2025_FN_Q65`, `2023_FN_Q20`, `2022_FN_Q16`, `2021_FN_Q49`, `2020_FN_Q28`).

And when a static table fills up, **Dynamic Trie Hashing** splits ONLY overflowing buckets bit by bit (`2021_FN_Q49`)!
- **Finer points:**
  - **Chaining & Open-Addressing Drills (`2026_FN_Q24`, `2026_AN_Q30`, `2025_FN_Q65`, `2020_FN_Q28`):**

- **GATE-level items:** Tracing linear probing empty slots (`2026_FN_Q24`), longest chain (`2026_AN_Q30`), double hashing (`2025_FN_Q65`, `2020_FN_Q28`), dynamic bit-trie insertion order (`2021_FN_Q49`), adversary defense (`2023_FN_Q20`), and dual-hash expected load (`2022_FN_Q16`).

### Chapter 04 · Divide and Conquer

#### Lesson 04.1 · Divide-and-Conquer Design: Max Subarray, Fast Power, Strassen & Inversions
- **Tag:** `[MUST]` — Classic algorithm design pattern (`2026_AN_Q38`, `2019_FN_Q14`, `2017_FN_Q30`, plus Merge/Quick link).
- **Estimated reading time:** 65 minutes.
- **Prerequisites:** Lesson 01.3 (Master Theorem), Lesson 02.2 (Merge/Quick).
- **Beginner must-see:** **Divide & Conquer** breaks one scary big problem into **independent** smaller twins, solves them recursively, and glues the answers back together! Sometimes a smart Combine step (`Max Crossing Sum` in $O(n)$, `2019_FN_Q14`) solves a quadratic $O(n^2)$ puzzle in $O(n \log n)$ — and sometimes spotting a pattern cuts it even further down to $O(n)$ (**Kadane's Algorithm** or **Adjacent Check**, `2026_AN_Q38`)!
- **Finer points:**
  - **The 3 D&C Steps:** Divide -> Conquer -> Combine (`Merge Sort`, `Quicksort`, `Binary Search`, `Karatsuba`).
  - **Maximum Subarray Sum (`2019_FN_Q14`):**
    - D&C ($O(n \log n)$): $\max(\text{Left}, \text{Right}, \text{CrossMid})$ where CrossMid scans left from `mid` and right from `mid+1`.
    - Kadane's DP ($O(n)$): handle all-negative array edge case cleanly!
  - **Counting Inversions via Merge Sort ($O(n \log n)$):** Whenever `R[j] < L[i]`, add `(mid - i + 1)` inversions!
  - **Fast Modular Exponentiation ($O(\log n)$):** `x^n = (x^{n/2})^2` (even) or `x · (x^{(n-1)/2})^2` (odd).
  - **Strassen's Matrix Multiplication:** $T(n) = 7T(n/2) + \Theta(n^2) \implies \Theta(n^{\log_2 7}) \approx \Theta(n^{2.81})$ (vs standard $8T(n/2) = \Theta(n^3)$).
  - **Adjacent Difference / Quantifier Reduction (`2026_AN_Q38`):** Recognizing when $\forall i > j$ reduces to adjacent $i+1, i$ check in $\Theta(n)$!
  - **Closest Pair of Points (`[SHOULD]`):** $O(n \log n)$ strip check (7 neighbours in `d × 2d` box).
- **GATE-level items:** Computing exact Max Subarray Sum (`2019_FN_Q14`: `29`), simplifying $\forall i > j$ conditions to $\Theta(n)$ (`2026_AN_Q38`), matching paradigms (`2017_FN_Q30`), and Strassen's/Karatsuba recurrences.
- **Diagrams needed:** `ARRAY` + `FRAMES` (Max Subarray Left / Right / Crossing Midpoint scan, `2019_FN_Q14`); `COMPARE` (Standard 8-block vs Strassen 7-block matrix multiplication); `CALLTREE` (Fast power `3^{13}`).
- **Hand traces:** Tracing Kadane AND D&C CrossMid on `2019_FN_Q14` (`29`); checking `2026_AN_Q38` on `[1, 2, 4, 7, 11]` vs `[1, 3, 5, 6]`; counting inversions during Merge Sort of `[4, 1, 3, 2]`.
- **Common mistakes:** Scanning EVERY pair `(l, r)` across midpoint in Max Subarray ($O(n^2)$ instead of independent max-left + max-right $O(n)$!); forgetting Kadane's initialization on all-negative arrays; writing $\Theta(n^2)$ double loop for `2026_AN_Q38` instead of comparing adjacent differences!

#### Lesson 05.2 · Huffman Coding (Tie-Breaking & Worst Depth) & Optimal File Merge
- **Tag:** `[MUST]` — High-yield Greedy NAT favourite (`2021_AN_Q57`, `2017_AN_Q59`).
- **Estimated reading time:** 60 minutes.
- **Prerequisites:** DS `D9` (Min-Heap), Lesson 05.1.
- **Beginner must-see:** Why spend 8 bits on the letter `'e'` AND 8 bits on rare `'z'`? **Huffman Coding** builds a binary tree where frequent characters live near the root (short codes like `01`) and rare characters sit deep at the bottom (long codes like `1101`), while guaranteeing NO code is a prefix of another (**Prefix-Free Code**)!
- **Finer points:**
  - **Prefix-Free Property:** All characters sit strictly at **leaves** of the binary tree (no character is an ancestor of another -> unambiguous decoding!).
  - **Huffman Tree Algorithm ($O(n \log n)$ with Min-Heap, or $O(n)$ using 2 queues if frequencies are pre-sorted!):** Repeatedly pop two smallest nodes and merge with sum weight.
  - **Expected Encoded Length (`2017_AN_Q59`):** $\sum p_i \cdot \text{len}(c_i)$ (per char) × message length $N$ (`100` chars).
  - **Same-Frequency Tie-Breaking & Dictionary Constraints (`2021_AN_Q57`):**
    - Swapping two leaves at equal frequency keeps total cost identical while enforcing lexicographic tie-break (`len(earlier) <= len(later)`!).
  - **Worst-Case Huffman Tree Height:** Fibonacci frequencies (`1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, ...`) produce maximum skewed height $n - 1$!
  - **Optimal Merge Pattern ($O(n \log n)$):** Same Min-Heap greedy merge for combining sorted files of sizes $s_1, \dots, s_n$ (`cost = sum of all internal node weights` = $\sum f_i \cdot d_i$).
- **GATE-level items:** Computing expected encoded length for `100` characters (`2017_AN_Q59`: `225` bits); building optimal prefix code respecting same-frequency dictionary ordering (`2021_AN_Q57`: `23` bits); finding max code length under $n$ symbols.
- **Diagrams needed:** `TREE` + `FRAMES` (Min-heap merge sequence building Huffman tree step by step, plus leaf-swap fixing dictionary length constraint in `2021_AN_Q57`); `TREE` (Fibonacci-frequency maximally skewed Huffman tree).
- **Hand traces:** Tracing `abbccdddee` (`2021_AN_Q57` -> initial `c=2,e=2,d=2,a=3,b=3` violates `b <= c`, swapped to `b=2,c=2,d=2,a=3,e=3` -> `23`!); tracing Optimal Merge on `[20, 30, 10, 5, 30]`.
- **Common mistakes:** Putting characters at internal nodes (breaks prefix-free property!); ignoring extra tie-breaking rules like Condition 2 in `2021_AN_Q57`; forgetting to multiply single-char average bits by message length (`100` in `2017_AN_Q59`!).

#### Lesson 06.2 · Classic DP Families (`LIS`, `0/1 Knapsack`, `LCS`, `Coin Change` & `Matrix Chain`)
- **Tag:** `[MUST]` — Covers `2024_AN_Q11`, `2018_FN_Q41`, `2018_FN_Q58(V_opt)`, and core GATE DP patterns.
- **Estimated reading time:** 90 minutes.
- **Prerequisites:** Lesson 06.1.
- **Beginner must-see:** Almost every GATE DP puzzle is a disguised cousin of ONE of five templates: **LIS** (keep longest sorted chain, `2024_AN_Q11`!), **0/1 Knapsack** (take or skip item inside weight limit, `2018_FN_Q58`!), **Coin Change** (ways/min coins), **LCS** (align two strings), or **Matrix Chain** (where to split parentheses `A_1..A_n`, `2018_FN_Q41`!)!
- **Finer points:**
  - **LIS / LNDS & Minimum Replacements (`2024_AN_Q11`):** $L[i] = 1 + \max_{j < i, A[j] \le A[i]} L[j]$ ($O(n^2)$ DP, $O(n \log n)$ patience sorting); minimum elements to replace = **$n - \text{LNDS}(A)$** (`2024_AN_Q11`: `7 - 4 = 3`)!
  - **0/1 Knapsack & Subset Sum:** $K[i][w] = \max(K[i-1][w], v_i + K[i-1][w - w_i])$; **$O(nW)$ is Pseudo-Polynomial** (exponential in bit-length $\log W$!); 1-D space optimization iterating `w` backwards from `W` down to `w_i`!
  - **Coin Change (`Min Coins` vs `Number of Combinations` vs `Permutations`):** Loop order (`coins outer` = combinations vs `sum outer` = permutations!).
  - **Longest Common Subsequence (LCS) & Edit Distance:** $O(mn)$ table (`match -> 1 + diag`, `mismatch -> max(up, left)`) + backtracking arrows to print actual LCS.
  - **Matrix Chain Multiplication (`2018_FN_Q41`):**
    - $m[i][j] = \min_{i \le k < j} (m[i][k] + m[k+1][j] + p_{i-1} p_k p_j)$ ($O(n^3)$ time, $O(n^2)$ space).

### Chapter 07 · Graph Traversals and DFS Applications

#### Lesson 07.1 · BFS, DFS, Graph Representations, Level Properties & Tree Diameter (`2-BFS`)
- **Tag:** `[MUST]` — Foundation for all graph algorithms (`2025_AN_Q29`, `2025_AN_Q59`, `2024_FN_Q37`, `2021_AN_Q25`, `2018_FN_Q40(II)`, `2017_AN_Q13`).
- **Estimated reading time:** 75 minutes.
- **Prerequisites:** DS `D3` (Stack), `D4` (Queue), `D12` (Graph Representations).
- **Beginner must-see:** Exploring an unknown maze has two natural styles: **BFS (Breadth-First Search)** uses a **Queue** to check every neighbour 1 step away, then 2 steps away, like ripples on water (automatically giving **unweighted shortest paths**!). **DFS (Depth-First Search)** uses a **Stack / Recursion** to dive blindly down one corridor until it hits a wall, then backtracks!
- **Finer points:**
  - **Time Complexity Trap:** Adjacency List = **$\Theta(V + E)$**; Adjacency Matrix = **$\Theta(V^2)$**!
  - **Connected Components & Forest Edge Count (`2024_FN_Q37`, `2025_AN_Q29(D)`):** An undirected graph on $V$ vertices whose BFS/DFS spanning forest has $E_F$ edges MUST have **$c = |V| - |E_F|$ connected components** (`100 - 40 = 60`, `2024_FN_Q37`)!
  - **BFS Level-Difference Property (`2025_AN_Q29(C)`, `2018_FN_Q40(II)`):**
    - For ANY non-tree edge `(u, v)` in an **undirected** BFS tree, **`|dist(u) - dist(v)| <= 1`** (can be `0` on a triangle $K_3$ -> so `= 1` in `2018_FN_Q40(II)` is FALSE!).
    - **Bipartite / Odd-Cycle Test:** Graph is bipartite $\iff$ NO same-level edge (`dist(u) == dist(v)`) exists in BFS!
  - **Valid BFS vs DFS Orders (`2021_AN_Q25`, `2017_AN_Q13`):** Checking valid queue/stack orders when adjacency list neighbour orders vary (first 3 BFS = level 0 + level 1, whereas first 3 DFS = depth 0, 1, 2, `2021_AN_Q25`!).
  - **Tree Diameter via 2-BFS (`2025_AN_Q59`):** BFS #1 from any vertex `v` finds farthest `u`; BFS #2 from `u` finds farthest `z`; `dist(u, z)` = exact **Tree Diameter**! (Warn: works on trees, FAILS on general graphs with cycles!).
- **GATE-level items:** Connected components from forest edges (`2024_FN_Q37`), undirected BFS level difference `<= 1` vs `= 1` (`2025_AN_Q29`, `2018_FN_Q40`), valid BFS sequences (`2017_AN_Q13`), `|BFS_3 - DFS_3|` (`2021_AN_Q25`), and 2-BFS tree diameter (`2025_AN_Q59`).
- **Diagrams needed:** `GRAPHPIC` + `FRAMES` (BFS layer ripples with Queue vs DFS path diving with Stack); `GRAPHPIC` (Triangle $K_3$ showing same-level edge `|1 - 1| = 0`, breaking `|i - j| = 1`); `TREE` (2-BFS Diameter finding `v -> u -> z`, `2025_AN_Q59`).
- **Hand traces:** Tracing `2021_AN_Q25` on 7-node complete binary tree (`|A - B| = 1`); tracing 2-BFS on an asymmetric 7-node tree; checking BFS bipartite 2-colouring on `C_5`.
- **Common mistakes:** Claiming `|depth(u) - depth(v)| = 1` instead of `<= 1` for undirected BFS non-tree edges (`2018_FN_Q40(II)`!); forgetting `O(V^2)` complexity when graph uses an Adjacency Matrix; applying 2-BFS diameter blindly to cyclic graphs.

#### Lesson 07.2 · DFS Timestamps, Edge Types, Topological Sort, SCC & Articulation Points
- **Tag:** `[MUST]` — High-frequency 2-mark MSQ/NAT unit (`2026_FN_Q50`, `2024_FN_Q60`, `2021_FN_Q39`, `2021_AN_Q30`, `2021_AN_Q19`, `2018_FN_Q40(I)`).
- **Estimated reading time:** 85 minutes.
- **Prerequisites:** Lesson 07.1.
- **Beginner must-see:** Give DFS a ticking clock `t` that stamps **`d[u]` (entry time, turns Grey)** and **`f[u]` (exit time, turns Black)**! Those two numbers act like matching brackets `( ... )` that immediately tell you whether a graph has a cycle (**Back Edge** to an active Grey node!), how to sort tasks in order (**Topological Sort**), and which single router failure disconnects the whole network (**Articulation Point**)!
- **Finer points:**
  - **Parenthesis Theorem & Nesting (`2026_FN_Q50`):** For any $u, v$, `[d[u], f[u]]` and `[d[v], f[v]]` are either **strictly nested** OR **completely disjoint** — NEVER partially overlapping (`2026_FN_Q50(D)` impossible!).
  - **4 Directed Edge Types (for edge `u -> v`):**
    1. **Tree Edge** (`v` White -> `d[u] < d[v] < f[v] < f[u]`, `2026_FN_Q50(A)`).
    2. **Back Edge** (`v` Grey -> **`d[v] < d[u] < f[u] < f[v]`** -> **CYCLE exists!** Impossible in a DAG! `2026_FN_Q50(B)`).
    3. **Forward Edge** (`v` Black & `d[u] < d[v] < f[v] < f[u]`).
    4. **Cross Edge** (`v` Black & `d[v] < f[v] < d[u] < f[u]`, `2026_FN_Q50(C)`!).
  - **Undirected DFS Edge Theorem (`2025_AN_Q29(B)`, `2018_FN_Q40(I)`):** An undirected simple graph has **ONLY Tree & Back edges** — **NO Cross edges and NO distinct Forward edges**!
  - **Directed Graph where `T` is BOTH BFS & DFS Tree (`2024_FN_Q60`):** **NO Forward edges** can exist (a forward edge jumps $\ge 2$ BFS depths, impossible in BFS!), whereas **Back edges and Cross edges CAN exist** (`2024_FN_Q60(C)`)!
  - **Topological Sort & DAG DP (`2021_AN_Q30`, `2021_AN_Q19`):** Exists $\iff$ graph is a **DAG** (no back edges!); Kahn's in-degree=0 queue OR reverse DFS finishing times (`f[u]`); computing max-product/longest path on a DAG (`2021_AN_Q19`).
  - **Strongly Connected Components (Kosaraju / Tarjan) (`2021_AN_Q30`):** 2-DFS method ($G$ then $G^T$ in decreasing $f[u]$ order); every vertex is trivially an SCC of size $\ge 1$!
  - **Articulation Points & Bridges (`2021_FN_Q39`):**
    - Root of DFS tree is an AP $\iff$ it has **$\ge 2$ children** in the DFS tree (`2021_FN_Q39(B)`).
    - Non-root `u` is an AP $\iff$ some DFS child `v` has **no back edge** jumping strictly above `u` (`low[v] >= d[u]`).
    - Leaf of DFS tree is NEVER an AP (`2021_FN_Q39(C)` false); an ancestor-descendant path CAN bypass an AP via another child's back edge (`2021_FN_Q39(D)` false!).
- **GATE-level items:** `[d[u], f[u]]` DAG edge inequalities (`2026_FN_Q50`), directed `BFS = DFS` tree edge elimination (`2024_FN_Q60`), Articulation Point MSQ (`2021_FN_Q39`), Topological Sort / Back Edges / SCCs (`2021_AN_Q30`), and DAG path product DP (`2021_AN_Q19`).
- **Diagrams needed:** `TIMELINE` + `GRAPHPIC` (White/Grey/Black DFS run with `[d[u], f[u]]` nesting bars + 4 edge classes); `GRAPHPIC` (Directed graph `v -> a, v -> b, b -> a` proving cross edges survive when BFS tree = DFS tree, `2024_FN_Q60`); `TREE` (AP Root $\ge 2$ children & non-root escape-rope back edge bypassing `u`, `2021_FN_Q39`).
- **Hand traces:** Tracing `d[u], f[u]` on a 5-node DAG (`2026_FN_Q50`), running Kahn's Topological Sort, and computing SCCs via Kosaraju.
- **Common mistakes:** Thinking a directed graph with `BFS tree = DFS tree` has NO edges outside `T` (`2024_FN_Q60(D)`!); thinking any path from ancestor `x` to descendant `y` must pass through AP `u` (`2021_FN_Q39(D)`!); thinking a graph without a cycle has "0 strongly connected components" (every single node is an SCC of size 1, `2021_AN_Q30(C)`!).

### Chapter 08 · Minimum Spanning Trees

#### Lesson 08.1 · Kruskal's & Prim's Algorithms, Complexities & Negative Edge Weights
- **Tag:** `[MUST]` — Foundation for `13/73` MST PYQs (`2025_FN_Q18`, `2020_FN_Q56`).
- **Estimated reading time:** 60 minutes.
- **Prerequisites:** DS `D9` (Binary Heaps), `D13` (Disjoint Sets / Union-Find).
- **Beginner must-see:** You want to connect $n$ cities with fiber cable using the **lowest total cable cost** (`n - 1` edges, zero cycles)! **Kruskal's** sorts ALL roads globally by cheapest price and keeps adding any road that doesn't create a loop (`Union-Find`); **Prim's** grows ONE single tree out from a starting city by always grabbing the cheapest bridge crossing into unvisited cities!
- **Finer points:**
  - **Kruskal's Algorithm:** Sort edges + Disjoint-Set (`O(E log E)` = `O(E log V)` — great for **sparse** or **pre-sorted** graphs!).
  - **Prim's Algorithm:** Binary Heap `O((V + E) log V)`, Fibonacci Heap `O(E + V log V)`, **Adjacency Matrix Array `O(V^2)`** (best for **dense** graphs $E \approx V^2$!).
  - **Negative Edge Weights Work!** Both Prim's and Kruskal's handle negative edges effortlessly (every spanning tree has exact $n - 1$ edges, so adding a constant makes all weights positive without changing any MST!).
  - **Shortest Path in $G$ vs MST Path (`2025_FN_Q18`):** MST minimizes total tree weight (and minimax bottleneck edge on paths), NOT pairwise path sums -> **`d_G(u, v) <= d_MST(u, v)`** always!
  - **Implicit Complete Graph Trick (`2020_FN_Q56`):** Weight `|i - j|` on $K_{100}$ uses only the 99 weight-1 edges `(v_i, v_{i+1})` -> weight `99`!
- **GATE-level items:** MST total weight on patterned complete graphs (`2020_FN_Q56`), `d_G(u,v) <= d_MST(u,v)` (`2025_FN_Q18`), Prim vs Kruskal time complexities across dense vs sparse representations.
- **Diagrams needed:** `GRAPHPIC` + `FRAMES` (Prim's growing blob vs Kruskal's merging forest step-by-step on the exact same 6-node graph); `COMPARE` (Kruskal vs Prim Binary Heap vs Prim Matrix + `d_G` vs `d_MST` triangle).
- **Hand traces:** Tracing both Kruskal's (with Union-Find `parent[]`) and Prim's (key array updates) on a 6-node graph; verifying `2020_FN_Q56` (`99`).
- **Common mistakes:** Thinking Prim's/Kruskal's break on negative edge weights; confusing Prim's key (`w(u, v)`) with Dijkstra's key (`dist[u] + w(u, v)`); assuming MST shortest path = graph shortest path (`2025_FN_Q18`).

#### Lesson 08.2 · MST Theorems (`Cut`, `Cycle`), Uniqueness, Counting MSTs & Edge Updates
- **Tag:** `[MUST]` — Highest-density 2-mark proof/counting trap unit (`2026_FN_Q49`, `2025_AN_Q37`, `2025_FN_Q64`, `2024_AN_Q53`, `2022_FN_Q49`, `2021_AN_Q64`, `2021_FN_Q48`, `2020_FN_Q39`, `2019_FN_Q44`, `2018_FN_Q57`, `2017_FN_Q48`).
- **Estimated reading time:** 85 minutes.
- **Prerequisites:** Lesson 08.1.
- **Beginner must-see:** Every MST proof in GATE boils down to ONE golden rule: **"Add a cheaper edge to create a cycle, then kick out a heavier edge on that cycle!"** From that single rule come the Cut Property, Cycle Property, Unique-Weight Uniqueness, AND how to check a new edge in $O(V)$ time!
- **Finer points:**
  - **Cut Property (`2022_FN_Q49(C)`, `2026_FN_Q49(D)`):** For any cut $(S, V \setminus S)$, the **unique lightest crossing edge** MUST be in every MST (special case $S = \{v\}$: lightest edge incident on EVERY vertex $v$ is in every MST when weights are distinct, `2026_FN_Q49(D)`!).
  - **Cycle Property (`2026_FN_Q49(A)`):** In any cycle $C$, the **unique heaviest edge** can NEVER be in any MST! (Beware converse: lightest edge of a cycle does NOT have to be in the MST if lighter edges connect those vertices outside `C`, `2026_FN_Q49(B)`!).
  - **Small-Edge Guarantees (`2022_FN_Q49(A, B)`):** With distinct weights, **1st & 2nd smallest** edges are ALWAYS in the MST; at least **one of 3rd & 4th smallest** is ALWAYS in the MST!
  - **Uniqueness Conditions (`2021_AN_Q64`, `2019_FN_Q44`, `2017_FN_Q48`):**
    - Distinct edge weights $\implies$ Unique MST (converse is FALSE: tied weights can STILL have a unique MST!).
    - Necessary & Sufficient: For EVERY cut, unique min crossing edge (or equivalently, cycle property holds strictly!).
  - **Constant Weight Offset (`2025_AN_Q37`):** Adding $\alpha$ to ALL edge weights shifts EVERY spanning tree by $(n - 1)\alpha$ -> **ALL MSTs remain identical!**
  - **Counting Distinct MSTs (`2024_AN_Q53`, `2021_FN_Q48`, `2018_FN_Q57`) & Keeping Edge `(B, C)` (`2025_FN_Q64`):**
    - Kruskal equal-weight layers: replace edges of weight $w$ inside same component structure, multiplying spanning ways across weight tiers!
    - Edge `(B, C)` is in *every* MST iff `x` is strictly smaller than the heaviest edge on EVERY alternative `B..C` path (`max_weight` bottleneck, `2025_FN_Q64`)!
  - **Dynamic MST Check on Adding 1 Edge `(u, v)` (`2020_FN_Q39`):** Find unique `u..v` path inside $T$ via DFS/BFS in **$\Theta(|V|)$**; $T$ remains an MST iff `w(u, v) >= max edge weight on u..v path in T`!
- **GATE-level items:** Cycle/Cut/Vertex-incident MSQs (`2026_FN_Q49`, `2022_FN_Q49`), counting distinct MSTs (`2024_AN_Q53`, `2021_FN_Q48`, `2018_FN_Q57`), max `x` for mandatory MST edge (`2025_FN_Q64`), $+\alpha$ offset (`2025_AN_Q37`), and $\Theta(|V|)$ new-edge verification (`2020_FN_Q39`).
- **Diagrams needed:** `FRAMES` (Exchange Argument proving Cut Property & Cycle Property); `GRAPHPIC` (Counterexample to `lightest edge in every cycle is in MST` & `heaviest incident edge is never in MST` via degree-1 bridge, `2026_FN_Q49`); `GRAPHPIC` (Equal-weight substitution multiplying MST counts `3 × 3 = 9`); `GRAPHPIC` (New edge `(u,v)` creating 1 cycle in `T`, `2020_FN_Q39`).
- **Hand traces:** Tracing `2022_FN_Q49(A,B)` on $K_3$ + 4th edge; tracing max bottleneck `u..v` path to bound `x` (`2025_FN_Q64` style); checking `2020_FN_Q39` in $\Theta(|V|)$.
- **Common mistakes:** Thinking "lightest edge in every cycle must be in MST" (`2026_FN_Q49(B)`!); thinking "distinct weights $\iff$ unique MST" (if-only, NOT if-and-only-if!); confusing $O(|E|)$ or $O(|E|\log|V|)$ when checking a new edge against an ALREADY built tree $T$ (`T` only has $|V|-1$ edges -> $\Theta(|V|)$, `2020_FN_Q39`!).

### Chapter 09 · Shortest Paths

#### Lesson 09.1 · Single-Source Shortest Paths: DAG Topo-Relax, Dijkstra & Bellman-Ford (`d_k(u)`)
- **Tag:** `[MUST]` — Core graph algorithms (`2026_AN_Q37`, `2026_FN_Q41`, `2025_AN_Q37`, `2017_FN_Q48(II)`).
- **Estimated reading time:** 80 minutes.
- **Prerequisites:** Lesson 07.2 (Topological Sort), DS `D9` (Min-Heap).
- **Beginner must-see:** How you find the fastest route from `s` depends 100% on the road weights! Unweighted? Plain **BFS** ($\Theta(V+E)$). No cycles (**DAG**)? Sort topologically and relax once (**$\Theta(V+E)$**, even with negative weights! `2026_AN_Q37`). All weights $\ge 0$? **Dijkstra** ($O((V+E)\log V)$). Negative edges allowed? **Bellman-Ford** ($O(VE)$)!
- **Finer points:**
  - **Unweighted BFS (`O(V+E)`) vs DAG Shortest Path (`O(V+E)`, `2026_AN_Q37`):** Topological Sort + 1 pass relaxing outgoing edges (`2026_AN_Q37`!).
  - **Dijkstra's Algorithm:** Greedy vertex extraction (`O(V^2)` matrix, `O((V+E)\log V)` binary heap); **FAILS on negative edges** (show 3-node counterexample!).
  - **Bellman-Ford & $k$-Hop DP `d_k(u)` (`2026_FN_Q41`):**
    - `d_k(u)` = min weight of path $s \to u$ using $\le k$ edges.
    - Monotonicity: **$d_{k+1}(u) \le d_k(u)$ ALWAYS** (`2026_FN_Q41(S1)`).
    - Failure of `d_k(u) <= d_k(v)` on edge `(u, v)` (`2026_FN_Q41(S2)`): breaks BOTH on multi-hop paths (where $d_1(v)$ exists via direct edge yet $d_1(u) = \infty$!) AND on negative-weight edges!
    - $V - 1$ passes find all shortest paths; **$V$-th pass** detects negative-weight cycles!
  - **Shortest Path Pitfalls (`2025_AN_Q37`, `2017_FN_Q48`):**
    - Adding constant $+\alpha$ to all edges CAN flip shortest paths (multi-hop paths pay $k\alpha$ penalty!).
    - Distinct positive edge weights do **NOT** guarantee unique shortest paths (`1 + 2 = 3` vs `3`? With distinct weights: `2 + 5 = 7` vs `1 + 6 = 7`!).
- **GATE-level items:** Fastest SSSP on DAG (`2026_AN_Q37`: $\Theta(m + n)$), Bellman-Ford $d_k(u)$ properties (`2026_FN_Q41`), $+\alpha$ weight shift (`2025_AN_Q37`), and distinct-weight shortest path ties (`2017_FN_Q48(II)`).
- **Diagrams needed:** `COMPARE` (5-row Shortest Path Selector: BFS / DAG-Topo / Dijkstra / Bellman-Ford / Floyd-Warshall); `FRAMES` (Dijkstra failing on `A->B=4, A->C=2, B->C=-5`); `GRAPHPIC` (Counterexamples breaking `2026_FN_Q41(S2)` and `2025_AN_Q37` & `2017_FN_Q48(II)`).
- **Hand traces:** Tracing DAG Topo-Relax on a 5-node DAG; tracing Bellman-Ford $d_0..d_3$ on a 4-node graph with negative edges; verifying `2026_FN_Q41(S1, S2)`.
- **Common mistakes:** Saying Dijkstra takes $\Theta(m + n)$ on a DAG (`2026_AN_Q37`!); thinking `d_k(u) <= d_k(v)` holds along a shortest-path edge `(u, v)` for ALL `k` (`2026_FN_Q41(S2)`!); claiming distinct edge weights make shortest paths unique (`2017_FN_Q48(II)`!).

#### Lesson 09.2 · All-Pairs Shortest Paths (`Floyd-Warshall`) & Johnson's Potential Reweighting
- **Tag:** `[MUST]` — Covers `2020_FN_Q57` and standard APSP ($O(V^3)$).
- **Estimated reading time:** 55 minutes.
- **Prerequisites:** Lesson 06.1 (DP), Lesson 09.1.
- **Beginner must-see:** To find shortest distances between *every* pair $(i, j)$, **Floyd-Warshall** gradually unlocks intermediate pit-stops `k = 1, 2, ..., n` (`can i -> j get faster by stopping at k?`). And if a sparse graph has negative edges, **Johnson's Reweighting** shifts edge weights cleanly using `w(u,v) + f(u) - f(v)` so ALL edges become $\ge 0$ without changing a single shortest path (`2020_FN_Q57`)!
- **Finer points:**
  - **Floyd-Warshall (`\Theta(V^3)` time, `\Theta(V^2)` space):**
    - `D[i][j] = min(D[i][j], D[i][k] + D[k][j])`.
    - Why **`k` MUST be the outermost loop**; negative cycle detection via `D[i][i] < 0`; **Warshall's Transitive Closure** (`||` and `&&`).
  - **Johnson's Reweighting Invariance (`2020_FN_Q57`):**
    - Define $w'(u, v) = w(u, v) + f(u) - f(v)$ for **ANY arbitrary function $f: V \to \mathbb{R}$** (even negative $f$!).
    - Telescoping proof: for any $s \to t$ path $P$, $w'(P) = w(P) + f(s) - f(t)$ -> preserves ALL shortest paths (and cycles!) **for EVERY $f$** (`2020_FN_Q57(A)`!).
    - Why Johnson's specifically sets $f(u) = \text{BellmanFord}(s_0, u)$? ONLY to make all $w'(u, v) \ge 0$ so Dijkstra can run in $O(VE + V^2 \log V)$, NOT because other `f` change shortest paths!
- **GATE-level items:** Proving why `w' = w + f(u) - f(v)` preserves shortest paths for **every** function $f$ (`2020_FN_Q57`); Floyd-Warshall step matrix updates and negative-cycle diagonal check.
- **Diagrams needed:** `MATRIX` + `FRAMES` (Floyd-Warshall `k = 1, 2, 3` matrix relaxation updates); `GRAPHPIC` (Telescoping potential cancellation along $s \to u_1 \to u_2 \to t$, `2020_FN_Q57`).
- **Hand traces:** Running Floyd-Warshall on a 4-vertex directed weighted graph; verifying `w'(P) = w(P) + f(s) - f(t)` with negative `f` values on two competing paths (`2020_FN_Q57`).
- **Common mistakes:** Picking `2020_FN_Q57(D)` ("if and only if `f(u)` is Bellman-Ford distance from dummy `s`") — missing that `f(u)` from dummy `s` is only needed to ensure `w' >= 0` for Dijkstra, whereas shortest-path preservation holds for **EVERY** $f$! Putting `k` as the innermost loop in Floyd-Warshall!

- **Lesson 01.1 (`Asymptotic Notations & Space Complexity`):**
  1. Let $f(n) = n^{\sin n}$? No — standard deterministic trap: let $f(n) = n^{1 + \sin(\pi n/2)}$ vs $g(n) = n$: which of $f = O(g)$, $f = \Omega(g)$, $f = \Theta(g)$ hold? (`MSQ`).
  2. Contrast Worst-Case Time vs Auxiliary Space Complexity of Merge Sort, In-place Quicksort, and Iterative Binary Search (`MSQ`).
  3. Which identities hold for positive functions: $f(n) + g(n) = \Theta(\max(f(n), g(n)))$, $2^{f(n)} = O(2^{g(n)})$ when $f(n) = O(g(n))$, $\log(f(n)) = \Theta(\log(g(n)))$? (`MSQ`).
- **Lesson 01.2 (`Loop Complexity Analysis`):**
  1. Find the tight $\Theta$ bound of `for(i=2; i<=n; i=i*i) for(j=1; j<=i; j++)` (`MCQ`).
  2. Count exact `printf` executions in `for(i=1; i<=64; i*=2) for(j=1; j<=i; j++)` (`NAT`).
  3. Find worst-case time complexity of checking if an array of $n$ integers contains any duplicate using two nested loops with early `return` (`MCQ`).
- **Lesson 01.3 (`Master Theorem, Recursion Trees & Substitutions`):**
  1. Solve $T(n) = 4 T(n/2) + n^2 \log n$ (Extended Master Case 2) (`MCQ`).
  2. Solve $T(n) = T(n/3) + T(2n/3) + c n$ using a Recursion Tree (`MCQ`).
  3. Compute the exact amortized cost per `INCREMENT` over $2^k$ calls on a $k$-bit binary counter (`MCQ`).

### Chapter 02 · Searching, Sorting and Selection
- **Lesson 02.1 (`Binary Search, Bitonic & Selection`):**
  1. Given a rotated sorted array `[15, 18, 22, 3, 6, 9, 11]`, find worst-case comparisons to locate minimum element across $n$ distinct rotated items (`MCQ`).
  2. Compare expected vs worst-case complexity of Randomized Quickselect vs Deterministic BFPRT Selection on $n$ elements (`MSQ`).
  3. Explain why Ternary Search (`mid1, mid2`) requires more worst-case comparisons ($\approx 2 \log_3 n$) than Binary Search (`\log_2 n`) (`MCQ`).
- **Lesson 02.2 (`Comparison Sorts`):**
  1. Count exact inversions in `[8, 4, 2, 1, 5, 3]` and match to swaps in Insertion Sort (`NAT`).
  2. In Quicksort on `[10, 20, 30, 40, 50, 60]` using Hoare vs Lomuto partition with pivot = last element, count partition recursive calls (`NAT`).
  3. Which sorting algorithms preserve Stability AND achieve $O(n \log n)$ worst-case time (`MSQ`)?
- **Lesson 02.3 (`Lower Bounds, Linear Sorts & k-Way Merge`):**
  1. Find the minimum worst-case comparisons required by any comparison-based algorithm to sort 5 distinct integers (`NAT`).
  2. Sort $n$ integers each lying in range $[0, n^3 - 1]$ in optimal $O(n)$ worst-case time using Radix Sort in base $n$: how many passes are needed? (`NAT`).
  3. Compute worst-case time complexity of merging $k$ sorted arrays of size $n/k$ each using a Min-Heap vs sequential pairwise merging (`MSQ`).

### Chapter 03 · Hashing (Algorithmic Focus)
- **Lesson 03.1 (`Probing, Universal & Dynamic Trie Hashing`):**
  1. Insert 7 keys using `h_1(k) = k mod 10` and `h_2(k) = 1 + (k mod 7)` into a table of size 10 — explain why `h_2(k) = 4` fails to reach all slots (`MCQ`).
  2. Show why a hash family $\mathcal{H} = \{h_{a,b}(x) = ((ax + b) \bmod p) \bmod m\}$ is universal and protects against adversarial inputs (`MSQ`).
  3. Given a 4-bucket Dynamic Trie hash state after 5 insertions, determine which new key forces a depth-2 binary split (`MCQ`).

### Chapter 04 · Divide and Conquer
- **Lesson 04.1 (`D&C: Max Subarray, Fast Power, Strassen & Inversions`):**
  1. Count exact number of multiplications made by Recursive Squaring `power(x, 29)` (`NAT`).

  3. Compare asymptotic time complexities of Standard Matrix Multiply, Strassen's ($7$ products), and Karatsuba's integer multiply ($3$ products) (`MSQ`).

### Chapter 05 · Greedy Algorithms
- **Lesson 05.1 (`Greedy Proofs, Activity Selection & Job Sequencing`):**
  1. Given 6 overlapping lecture intervals `[1,4], [2,5], [3,6], [5,7], [4,8], [6,9]`, find minimum number of lecture halls needed (`NAT`).
  2. Schedule 5 jobs with `(deadline, profit)` `[(2, 60), (1, 100), (3, 20), (2, 40), (1, 20)]` to maximize profit (`NAT`).
  3. Give a small 3-activity counterexample where Greedy "Shortest Duration First" fails Activity Selection (`MCQ`).
- **Lesson 05.2 (`Huffman Coding & Optimal Merge`):**
  1. Six symbols have Fibonacci frequencies `1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8`: find the length of the longest Huffman codeword (`NAT`).

### Chapter 06 · Dynamic Programming
- **Lesson 06.1 (`DP Foundations, Loop Orders & Rod Cutting`):**
  1. Given `dp[i][j] = dp[i+1][j] + dp[i][j-1]`, identify which nested loop directions (`i` up/down, `j` up/down) compute the table correctly (`MSQ`).

- **Lesson 06.2 (`Classic DP: LIS, Knapsack, LCS, Coin Change & MCM`):**

  2. Given coins `{1, 3, 4}` and target `6`, contrast Greedy choice (`4+1+1 = 3` coins) vs DP minimum coins (`3+3 = 2` coins) (`NAT`).
  3. Given 4 matrices $A_1(10\times 20), A_2(20\times 5), A_3(5\times 30), A_4(30\times 10)$, compute minimum scalar multiplications (`NAT`).

### Chapter 07 · Graph Traversals and DFS Applications
- **Lesson 07.1 (`BFS, DFS, Level Properties & 2-BFS Diameter`):**

### Chapter 08 · Minimum Spanning Trees
- **Lesson 08.1 (`Kruskal, Prim & Negative Weights`):**
  1. Run Kruskal's & Prim's on a 5-vertex graph containing negative edge weights (`-4, -2, 1, 3, 5`) and find MST weight (`NAT`).

  2. A connected graph has two cycles sharing no edges, each containing two tied minimum edges of weight `3`: find total distinct MSTs (`NAT`).
  3. If an existing edge `e` ALREADY inside MST `T` has its weight **increased** by `5`, what is the time complexity to check if `T` stays an MST? (`MCQ`).

  3. Given potential `f(u)` from Bellman-Ford on dummy source $s_0$, verify all reweighted edges $w'(u, v) = w(u, v) + f(u) - f(v)$ are non-negative (`NAT`).
