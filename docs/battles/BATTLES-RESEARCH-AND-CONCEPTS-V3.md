# Battles v3: research, then concepts beyond "you vs a rival"

18 Nov 2026. Owner: "don't just stick to pick-a-rival; research comprehensively and think out of the box."

## 1. What the research says works (sources at the end)

| Finding | Where it comes from | What it means for us |
|---|---|---|
| Short timed runs with a **personal best** are compulsive on their own, no opponent needed | Chess.com Puzzle Rush: 5 minutes, 3 strikes ends the run, puzzles get harder; the CEO "motivated by his own records" | A solo mode works even when no friend is online, which fixes the honest empty-lobby problem |
| **Losing still pays**, and a **monthly reset** gives everyone a fresh climb | Chess.com Puzzle Battle season points, win or lose; leaderboard resets monthly | Keep points for finishing (we already do), add short seasons |
| **Many modes on the same question set** keep it fresh without new content | Blooket / Gimkit: one set, 18–25 modes | One PYQ bank, several modes; no new content needed per mode |
| A **strategy layer** on top of answering adds thinking beyond recall | Gimkit: spend what you earn | Decisions between questions (which region to attack, when to use a skip) |
| **Co-op balances competitive pressure**; shared goals include weaker students | Duolingo Friends Quests; ZEP Quiz co-op (class total moves a shared goal); ASCD on co-op games | A team-vs-the-paper mode, not only player-vs-player |
| Streaks and leagues motivate but can push easy grinding and anxiety | Utrecht thesis on Duolingo (23k Reddit comments); small interview studies | Reward hard questions more; no punishing streak loss |
| **Scale + elimination** creates spectacle; big launches used **bots** to fill games | Trivia Royale (1,000 players → 32 knockout); bots at launch | Only when we have players; bots would have to be labelled (owner decision D11) |
| **Status, not prizes** | Trivia Royale "Royale Lounge" | Titles, frames, map ownership instead of cash |
| Indian exam apps rely on challenge-a-friend, streaks and leaderboards; no independent evidence they work | Tayari, Noxious listings; no peer-reviewed JEE/NEET study found | Differentiate on something they do not have |

## 2. The big idea: the GATE syllabus is the game board

Every GATE student already thinks in a map: subjects, topics, weightage. Make that the world.

**Syllabus Conquest** (the meta-game, always on screen)
- The CS syllabus drawn as a territory map: each subject is a region sized by its real GATE weightage (Programming and DS biggest, Calculus small), each topic is a tile.
- You capture a tile by proving it: a short run on that topic at a set accuracy. Tiles decay if you do not revisit them (spaced revision, disguised as defending your land).
- Your map is your real readiness, drawn as a world. Nothing invented; it reads the same data as analytics.
- Clubs share a map: a weekly contest for who holds each region (club points = tiles held).

**Three ways to play on that board**
1. **Rush** (solo, always available): Puzzle-Rush style on a region or the whole map. 5 minutes, three wrong ends the run, questions get harder, beat your best and your friends' bests. One **daily rush** with the same questions for everyone, shareable score card (a GATE "Wordle").
2. **Raid** (co-op): once a week a real past paper becomes the boss (for example "GATE CS 2024 Set 1"). Every correct answer by any member of your club or friend group hits it; 2-mark questions hit harder; the raid ends when the paper is cleared or the week ends. Everyone contributes, nobody loses to a friend.
3. **Duel** (versus, the existing friend battles): a contest for a tile or a region: whoever scores more on that topic takes the tile from the other's map. Live, later, or scheduled (all already built on the server).

**Why this is not "AI-generated game UI"**: the hero visual is a map that only a GATE product could have, sized by real data, and it changes every time the student studies.

## 3. Visual world (follows from the idea)
A surveyor's map on a drafting table: parchment and ink linework, regions in muted terrain colours (ochre, terracotta, olive, slate grey, sand), captured tiles stamped in a solid ink colour per player or club, contour lines for difficulty, a compass rose as the season clock. No purple, no blue. Fonts: a sturdy display face for region names, a monospace-free numeric face for figures.

## 4. Screens
- **Board (home)**: the map fills the screen; HUD with rating, coins, inbox; a bottom sheet with Rush / Raid / Duel.
- **Region view**: zoom into a subject; tiles, their state, "Rush this region", "Challenge a friend for a tile".
- **Run screen** (Rush, Raid, Duel share one in-game question screen): question card, option tiles, timer, strikes or the boss's health or the rival's score depending on mode.
- **Result**: tiles stamped onto the map with an animation; points and coins; rematch / play again.
- **Club**: the shared map and this week's raid boss.
- **Profile**: map summary, history, locker.

## 5. Decisions for the owner
1. Approve the Syllabus Conquest direction (map as the board; Rush, Raid, Duel as modes).
2. Tile decay (revision) on or off.
3. Raid boss = a real past paper each week (recommended) or a mixed set.
4. Daily rush shared publicly (score card only, no names) yes/no.
